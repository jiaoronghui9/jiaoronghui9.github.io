Product Overview

Feeding Denitrifying Bacteria, Hitting TN Limits

When wastewater is carbon-limited, denitrifying bacteria cannot convert nitrate to nitrogen gas — and total nitrogen in the effluent exceeds the permit. Sodium acetate is the preferred external carbon source: it is consumed faster and more completely than methanol (no acclimation period, no hazardous storage) and more reliably than complex sugars, giving stable denitrification in anoxic zones and deep-nitrogen-removal retrofits.

Our industrial grade sodium acetate carries 58-60% active content per HG/T 2031-2008, low moisture and low insolubles for clean dosing preparation. Municipal WWTPs upgrading for stricter TN discharge — increasingly common across Vietnam and Indonesia — are our core users, and we support jar-scale dosing trials with technical guidance.

FAQ

Sodium Acetate — Common Questions

Why sodium acetate instead of methanol or glucose as carbon source?
Methanol works but is hazardous to store, needs bacteria acclimation, and performs poorly at low temperature. Glucose is cheap but can favor unwanted biomass growth. Sodium acetate is non-hazardous, immediately bio-available, gives fast and stable denitrification rates, and is dosed with simple equipment — the reason most modern TN-removal retrofits specify it.
How much sodium acetate do I need per kg of nitrogen removed?
Theoretical COD demand is about 4-5 kg COD per kg NO3-N denitrified; in practice plants dose based on their anoxic-zone kinetics and effluent targets. Our technical team will help you calculate a dosing rate from your influent/effluent TN data — and starting with a trial ton lets your operators tune it on real water.
How should sodium acetate be stored?
Store in a cool, dry place in sealed bags — it is hygroscopic, so keep unopened bags away from humidity. Dissolved solutions should be prepared fresh or stored short-term with mild agitation to prevent biological growth. Shelf life is 2 years in original sealed packaging.
