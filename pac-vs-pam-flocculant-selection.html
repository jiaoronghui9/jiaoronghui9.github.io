PAC vs PAM: Understanding the Difference and Selecting a Treatment Program

PAC and PAM are both used in water and wastewater treatment, but they perform different functions. PAC is an inorganic coagulant; PAM is a polymer flocculant. The appropriate product and dosage depend on the water matrix, process configuration and treatment target.

What PAC does

Polyaluminum chloride (PAC) is an inorganic coagulant. It helps destabilize fine suspended particles and colloids so that they can aggregate during coagulation and subsequent clarification. PAC selection may involve product form (solid or liquid), Al₂O₃ content, basicity, pH and the applicable water-treatment requirements.

What PAM does

Polyacrylamide (PAM) is a water-soluble polymer used as a flocculant or conditioning aid. Depending on its ionic character and molecular characteristics, PAM can promote particle bridging, strengthen flocs or assist sludge dewatering. Common product categories include anionic PAM (APAM), cationic PAM (CPAM) and nonionic PAM (NPAM). They are not interchangeable; selection depends on the solids, charge characteristics and process.

Can PAC and PAM be used together?

In some treatment systems, PAC is added first for coagulation and PAM is added downstream to support floc formation. This sequence is not universal. Chemical compatibility, dosing points, mixing intensity, pH and retention time should be assessed in the actual process. Excessive or poorly timed polymer addition can impair clarification or increase operating cost.

How to select the right product

  • Identify the application: drinking-water treatment, industrial wastewater, municipal wastewater or sludge dewatering.
  • Record raw-water pH, turbidity or suspended solids, and any relevant contaminant or discharge target.
  • For PAM, identify whether the process needs anionic, cationic or nonionic character and whether a specific molecular-weight range is required.
  • Compare candidate products using jar tests or application-specific trials under representative operating conditions.
  • Evaluate treated-water quality, sludge characteristics, chemical consumption and total operating cost—not just the price per tonne.

Information to include in a product inquiry

Share the water source or wastewater type, current treatment process, target result, approximate consumption, product form and destination. If you do not know the required PAM type or grade, describe the process and the problem to be solved rather than guessing a specification.
