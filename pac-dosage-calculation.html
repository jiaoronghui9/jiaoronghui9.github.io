PAC Dosage Calculation: From Jar Test to Daily Consumption

A PAC consumption estimate requires two inputs: a dose established through testing and the volume of water treated. The calculation below converts those inputs into a planning estimate. It does not determine the correct dose for a particular water source.

Step 1 — establish a dose through jar testing

There is no universal PAC dosage. Raw-water turbidity, suspended solids, pH, temperature, organic matter, mixing and clarification conditions all affect performance. Test representative water samples across a suitable dose range, compare the treated-water results and confirm that the selected dose is repeatable under operating conditions. Do not use an example dose from another plant as a purchase specification.

Step 2 — convert dose and flow into daily consumption

For a dose expressed in mg/L and a flow expressed in m³/day:

Daily PAC (kg/day) = dose (mg/L) × flow (m³/day) ÷ 1,000

This relationship follows from the unit conversion between milligrams per litre and kilograms per cubic metre.

Worked example

If a plant treats 10,000 m³/day and the jar test supports a PAC dose of 30 mg/L:

30 × 10,000 ÷ 1,000 = 300 kg/day

For a 30-day operating month at the same flow and dose, the estimate is 9,000 kg, or 9 metric tonnes. Actual consumption will change if flow, raw-water quality, dose or operating days change.

Step 3 — estimate a purchasing quantity

Use the measured or validated dose, expected daily flow and operating days to estimate monthly consumption. Add any stock buffer only after considering lead time, storage capacity, product shelf life and seasonal changes in raw-water quality. Do not use an unverified online price as a current quotation; obtain a product- and destination-specific offer.

Factors that can change the result

  • Changes in turbidity, suspended solids or organic load.
  • Seasonal changes in raw-water temperature and composition.
  • pH and alkalinity conditions that affect coagulation.
  • Differences between PAC product forms and Al₂O₃ grades.
  • Mixing, flocculation, sedimentation and filtration performance.
  • Whether a compatible flocculant is used downstream, based on actual testing.

Information needed for a practical estimate

Provide the water source, flow rate, jar-test dose, operating days per month, PAC form and grade, and destination. If the dose has not yet been established, begin with representative water testing rather than relying on a generic dosage figure.
